Expense Policy Assistant Specification

Capstone proposal and implementation contract | Version 1.6 | 7 November 2026

Build a travel expense assistant for a six person beginner team. The assistant will answer domestic and international travel policy questions using retrieval augmented generation, read receipt images and compare expenses with policy limits and a declared trip budget, and search for flights and hotels without booking them.

The project uses the course supplied hosted small model, LangChain adapters and LangGraph workflow orchestration. No fine tuning or fallback to a larger model is planned. The fictional SPARKS AI Lab policy and 120 golden cases in this package define the initial behavior. This is a specification and test package, not a completed application or measured evaluation result.

Scope and user outcomes

Capability | Required result
Policy questions | Answer hotel, flight and local transport questions for domestic India and international US, UK and Singapore travel; cite applicable policy sections.
Receipt review | Extract visible fields from uploaded JPEG or PNG images, ask for confirmation, calculate claim totals, and distinguish budget, eligibility and readiness.
Travel recommendations | Search by route or destination and dates; shortlist up to three compliant flights or hotels with prices, evidence and freshness.
Explicit boundaries | No booking, payment, reservation changes, final claim approval or fraud determination. Meals and other destination countries require scope guidance or review.


Completion depends on three external capabilities

The supplied model must accept images, or the course must permit a separate OCR service. Typed transcription can support an interim demo but does not complete the image requirement. Dense hybrid search requires permitted embeddings. Live flight and hotel recommendations require an accessible travel search provider; fixture recommendations alone do not complete live search.


Functional requirements and acceptance

ID | Requirement and acceptance example
FR01 | Ask a policy question. Return a concise conclusion, conditions and source rule IDs; unknown dates or unsupported countries never receive invented limits.
FR02 | Upload up to five images. Validate bytes, MIME type and size; preserve per image provenance and show extracted fields for user correction.
FR03 | Confirm critical fields before final calculation. Missing currency, inconsistent totals or unclear dates trigger a specific clarification.
FR04 | Calculate independently of the model. Apply nightly, daily and per journey caps; include mandatory taxes and fees; preserve exclusions separately.
FR05 | Show claimed total, policy eligible amount, exclusions, over-cap amount, trip budget, remaining budget or excess, and evidence completeness.
FR06 | Search flights and hotels through a read-only adapter. Validate required inputs, normalize comparable all-in prices, filter policy, rank, and label freshness.
FR07 | Support follow-ups. A corrected total or changed date invalidates dependent calculations and search results; preserve unrelated confirmed fields.
FR08 | Provide policy citations and provenance. A citation must exist and substantively support the claim; model-generated URLs are not trusted.
FR09 | Reject booking requests, ignore document instructions, isolate sessions, redact sensitive traces and handle provider/JSON failures with bounded retries.


Example result using fictional policy

An E grade traveler spends INR 6000 per night for two nights in Bengaluru and INR 2000 on eligible local transport on one day. With confirmed evidence and an INR 15000 trip budget: claimed INR 14000; eligible INR 11500; over category caps INR 2500; budget WITHIN_BUDGET with INR 1000 remaining; compliance EXCEEDS_POLICY; readiness HUMAN_REVIEW. Being within the trip budget does not override category limits.


Architecture and portability

Streamlit → FastAPI validation and session service → route by request type. Simple policy questions use a short RAG answer path. Receipt reviews and travel recommendations use the LangGraph planner → executor tools → critic workflow with at most one repair. All model roles use the same configured hosted small model.

LangChain and LangGraph belong to the same ecosystem, so using both does not eliminate framework dependence. Keep the policy engine, monetary calculations, schemas and provider interfaces in plain Python. Framework-specific code belongs in adapters and orchestration modules. Test business logic without either framework.

Layer | Implementation
UI and API | Streamlit forms, image upload and evidence panels; FastAPI request validation using Pydantic.
Core | Plain Python policy engine using Decimal; versioned JSON rules and evidence identifiers.
Retrieval | BM25 keyword search plus Qdrant dense retrieval when permitted, rank fusion and bounded same-model reranking.
State and storage | SQLite for prototype sessions and checkpoints; private uploads isolated by session. Retention: raw images 24 hours, redacted traces 30 days, with owner-requested deletion. See Operational endpoints, error envelope and retention. Separate policy corpus.
Provider adapters | ModelPort, ReceiptParserPort, PolicyRetrieverPort, TravelSearchPort and FxRatePort. Use deterministic fake implementations in tests.
Evaluation | pytest for tools; JSON golden-case runner for complete flows; latency, token and quality logs. LangSmith supplies shared tracing, dataset experiments and feedback; verify account quotas and cost before use.


Tool contracts

retrieve_policy(query, applicability) → evidence[]; parse_receipt(file_id) → extraction; calculate_claim(confirmed_items, rule_ids, fx, budget) → assessment; search_flights(criteria) → offers[]; search_hotels(criteria) → offers[]. Policy applicability and authorization are validated by the server, not accepted blindly from model output.

Keep search requests read-only in business effect even when a provider uses HTTP POST for searching. Do not register order, payment, booking or cancellation endpoints. Provider credentials remain server-side.


How RAG BM25 and agents work together

RAG is the evidence workflow: retrieve applicable policy passages and give them to the model before it explains a policy conclusion. BM25 is a keyword-ranking method used inside retrieval. Agents decide which permitted steps to take and coordinate tools. These are complementary responsibilities, not three alternative architectures.

Use case | RAG responsibility | BM25 responsibility | Agent responsibility
Policy questions | Retrieve policy sections and support a cited answer. | Match policy IDs and terms such as economy, hotel, boarding pass and city names. | Simple questions use a fixed path; complex questions can invoke planning and bounded critique.
Receipt review | Retrieve rules for confirmed category, geography, grade and date. | Search relevant policy text from extracted facts; never read the image itself. | Coordinate parsing, confirmation, retrieval, calculations and evidence checks.
Travel recommendation | Retrieve limits and conditions to assess offers from the search API. | Find hotel and flight policy sections; never obtain live prices. | Coordinate provider search, clarification, rule selection and recommendation explanation.


Tasks performed by ordinary tools

Image extraction uses the supplied model’s vision capability or a permitted OCR adapter. Money calculations, date validation, FX rounding, caps, hard filters and ranking are deterministic Python. Provider APIs supply live prices and availability. SQLite stores state. None of these tasks becomes RAG merely because an agent calls it.

BM25 and semantic search

BM25 ranks passages using matching words, term frequency and document length. It does not understand images or enforce geographic rules. Normalize aliases such as Bangalore and Bengaluru before searching. Semantic vector search can help match paraphrases such as “getting from the airport” with “airport transfer.” Explicit metadata filters still enforce organization, dates and access on both retrieval paths.


Architecture for policy preparation

Run this flow when the policy corpus changes, not for every user question. Both indexes must refer to the same policy and chunk versions. The reviewed rule registry drives arithmetic and links back to evidence.

DIAGRAM DESCRIPTION: Policy documents are extracted into metadata-bearing chunks for BM25 and optional vector retrieval. A separate human-reviewed numeric rule registry feeds Python calculations.

The embedding branch is selected by configuration ([embeddings] in config/settings.toml). By user decision on 2026-11-10 (Option B) the prototype uses local FastEmbed embeddings (BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5, 384 dimensions, ONNX on CPU) as a stand-in until the hosted embedding API is supplied; the hosted endpoint replaces it by configuration alone. If embeddings are disabled or fail, use BM25 plus query expansion and same-model reranking and disclose bm25_only mode in every answer. Policy approval and version publication are human-controlled operations.


Architecture for user request processing

All three use cases reuse one policy retrieval service. LangGraph controls routing and pause/resume state; LangChain adapters expose model, retrieval and tool interfaces.

DIAGRAM DESCRIPTION: Validated requests branch into short policy RAG, receipt parsing and confirmation followed by policy calculation, or live travel search followed by policy filtering. Complex paths use a bounded critic before the final response.

Every clarification pauses execution until the user supplies the missing facts. The critic can request one executor repair. An unresolved issue then produces a clarification or review result. Booking and payment tools are absent.


Deterministic request routing

Routing is a pure function of the validated request and stored session state. The model never chooses the route. This keeps the simple-path and full-agent latency comparison meaningful and makes every run reproducible.

Evaluate these conditions in order and take the first match. One: an attached image, or a pending receipt confirmation on the session, routes to receipt review. Two: a travel search intent carrying a search type, route or destination with dates routes to travel search. Three: a correction or follow-up routes to whichever route produced the result it invalidates. Four: if retrieval returns equally authoritative conflicting rules for the asked applicability, the current route escalates to the planner and critic path. Five: everything else is a simple policy question.

The simple path runs clarify applicability, retrieve, generate and validate citations, with no planner call and no critic call. An escalation is recorded in the trace as route_escalated with the condition that triggered it, so the two latency populations stay separable in LangSmith.


Execution steps for the three use cases

UC01 Answer a policy question

1. Validate the session and identify the question. 2. Determine geography, grade and applicable date; ask only for missing facts needed to answer. 3. Call retrieve_policy. 4. Generate an answer from returned evidence. 5. Validate citation IDs and check claim support. Return a clarification or abstention if evidence is insufficient. Straightforward questions do not need separate planner and critic model calls.

Example: “What hotel limit applies to grade E in Bengaluru?” BM25 retrieves P02 and metadata selects the applicable version. The answer cites the INR 5000 nightly cap. A policy query that combines conflicting versions or several exceptions may use the fuller agent workflow.

UC02 Review an expense image

1. Planner identifies the expense-review task. 2. Executor calls the image parser and records uncertainty. 3. Pause for critical-field confirmation. 4. Retrieve applicable hotel, flight or local-transport policy through shared RAG. 5. Resolve evidence IDs to reviewed numeric rules. 6. Python computes eligibility and trip-budget status. 7. Critic checks that the explanation matches evidence and tool outputs. 8. Return the assessment, citations and missing evidence.

Example: one Bengaluru hotel night totals INR 5900 for grade E. P02 supplies the INR 5000 cap. Python reports INR 900 above the category limit. Budget status is calculated separately from the user’s supplied trip budget. No RAG operation is used to add the numbers or read the pixels.

UC03 Recommend flights and hotels

1. Planner gathers route or destination, dates, grade and traveler details. 2. Executor searches the approved provider and normalizes offers. 3. Retrieve applicable policy using the same RAG service; reuse valid cached evidence where appropriate. 4. Resolve reviewed rules and apply hard constraints, complete-price checks and ranking in Python. 5. Critic checks freshness, compliance claims and explanation. 6. Return up to three qualifying options with source, timestamp and citations. Offer information only; do not book.

Policy retrieval can also happen before the provider call to narrow the search. After candidate offers arrive, evaluate them against the same authoritative rule version. Missing baggage, taxes or FX produce provisional results rather than invented totals.

Shared retrieval contract

retrieve_policy accepts a query and a validated applicability context. Server-derived organization and access scope cannot be overridden by the model. It returns evidence with rule_id, document_id, version, section, text and source location. Required numeric decisions must resolve those IDs through the reviewed rule registry. Unknown or stale registry entries block final calculation.

Inside retrieval: filter → BM25 and optional vectors → reciprocal rank fusion and deduplication → bounded same-model reranking → evidence package. If reranking emits invalid IDs or malformed JSON, use the valid fused ranking. If vector retrieval is unavailable, report degraded retrieval mode and retain the lexical path. Never turn an outage into invented policy evidence.

State and agent boundaries

Persist intent, confirmed facts, missing fields, evidence IDs, tool results, draft, critique and repair count. Planner returns allowed tool names and required inputs. Executor invokes only registered tools with validated arguments. Critic returns PASS, REPAIR or CLARIFY with evidence references; it cannot approve claims, change numeric tool outputs, or expand permissions. A correction invalidates affected calculations and cached offers.

Verification additions

Verify each route uses shared policy evidence. Check that image parsing and provider search are not sent to BM25 as substitute operations. Measure BM25-only, semantic-only when available, and hybrid retrieval with the same gold rule references. Compare simple-path latency against full-agent latency. Confirm repair_count never exceeds one. All 120 existing cases remain NOT_RUN until the application is evaluated.


Retrieval and policy management

Ingest policy_sources only. Each section has a stable rule ID, organization, version, effective dates, geographic applicability and authority. Retain original text and section references; clean headings and preserve table headers and exceptions. Begin with section chunks of about 300 to 600 tokens and evaluate before tuning.

Filter by organization and access scope before retrieving. Apply service-date and geographic applicability while retaining general rules. Retrieve about 15 keyword and 15 vector candidates per focused query, fuse rankings, deduplicate, rerank a bounded shortlist with the supplied model, and send roughly 4 to 6 useful passages to generation. These counts are initial settings, not measured optima.

Use the same embedding model and settings for documents and queries. Document vectors are cached in SQLite keyed by model and text hash, so a provider or model change re-embeds automatically. Keyword and vector candidates are fused with reciprocal rank fusion (c = 60) after the access and applicability filters have been applied to both. Answers report the retrieval mode (hybrid or bm25_only). The local FastEmbed stand-in is a user-approved deviation; no other local embedding or reranking model may be introduced.

The 20 policy section files included here are a starter corpus. They are not a 500-document corpus. Week one must establish a legitimate route to the brief’s 500 to 2000 source documents: course supplied material or approved distinct policy-related references and historical cases. Do not inflate counts by splitting files. Keep cases non-authoritative and keep golden answers out of retrieval.

Rule governance

A human-reviewed rule registry mirrors the fictional policy. Numeric rules reference source IDs and versions. A policy revision invalidates dependent cached retrieval and rules until reviewed. Where authoritative sources conflict, stop and identify the conflict. A historical FAQ cannot override a current policy.

Generation contract

Return answer, evidence_ids, missing_fields and limitations. Treat documents as untrusted data: their instructions cannot modify tools or policy. Verify evidence IDs in code and sample-check support during evaluation. Retrieval scores and the critic’s confidence are not guarantees of correctness.


Reference data and the reviewed rule registry

policy_sources/rule_registry.json is the reviewed numeric rule registry this specification requires. It is the authority for every number used in a calculation. Application code must resolve a cap_id or parameter_id from the registry; a numeric policy literal in domain code is a defect. The registry currently holds 22 caps and 13 parameters, each carrying its rule_id, policy_version, effective window and, where the golden suite exercises it, the matching scenario group. Ten of the 22 caps have boundary coverage in the suite; the other twelve appear only in policy answers, so treat them as reviewed but untested numbers.

Each cap declares a conversion_order. A cap with before_conversion is compared against the line amount in the cap's own currency and the result is then converted, which is how the domestic and international hotel and local-transport caps work. A cap with after_conversion is compared in INR after documented conversion, which is how the P05 international flight caps work. Encoding this distinction removes the single prose sentence that previously carried it.

policy_sources/reference_data.json holds the enumerations. Supported countries are IN, US, UK and SG. UK is a package-local code used by policy 1.1 and every golden case; the ISO 3166 code is GB, which is accepted as an input alias and never emitted. Indian hotel caps use the metro tier for Bengaluru, Delhi and Mumbai and the other tier for every remaining Indian city. Aliases such as Bangalore and Bombay normalize before BM25 scoring, cap lookup and cache keys. The FX fixtures FX-DEMO-2026 and the GT110 card rate are recorded there with their dates; they are fictional and are not market quotes.

The same file defines the controlled vocabularies: readiness, compliance and budget status, offer policy status and price basis, critic decision, run status, reason codes, missing-field codes and error codes. Reason and missing-field codes carry the machine-readable decision while the response prose is generated separately and is what human review grades. No existing golden case asserts on reasons or missing_fields, so adopting these codes changes no expected answer.

Policy 1.1 is effective from 1 January 2026 through 31 December 2026 and P01 establishes no policy outside that window. Golden cases freeze their clocks inside it and are unaffected. A live demonstration after 31 December 2026 will correctly refuse to state a limit for a current service date, so before any such demonstration either publish a reviewed policy 1.2 with 2027 effective dates or run the demonstration against a documented fixed clock and label it.


Receipt processing and budget calculation

Validate file type and actual decoded image before storage. Send images only to the permitted vision-capable small model or approved OCR adapter. Extract visible text with image ID provenance, then show critical fields for confirmation. Confidence can prioritize review, but unreadable amounts remain null. Synthetic receipt fixtures test controlled inputs; real camera photos need a separate later evaluation set.

Record | Required fields
ClaimContext | session_id, organization_id, grade, home country, destination, trip dates, submission date, budget_inr and coverage_complete.
ReceiptExtraction | image_id, merchant, receipt_id, category, service dates, currency, components, payable_total, uncertainties and user_confirmed.
ExpenseLine | category, service_date, journey_id or hotel_night, amount, currency, excluded_amount, evidence_ids and fx reference.
Assessment | claimed_total_inr, eligible_total_inr, excluded_total_inr, over_cap_total_inr, budget_status, compliance_status, readiness and reasons.
Evidence | rule_id, document_id, version, section, source location and supporting excerpt.


First validate totals and dates. Deduplicate pending confirmation. Apply exclusions before category caps. Cap hotels per night, local travel per local day and flights per one way journey. Convert confirmed lines with documented FX and round per line to paise using ROUND_HALF_UP. Compare the full claimed total with the supplied budget separately. If the upload is incomplete, display a subtotal and UNKNOWN trip-budget status.

Readiness precedence: INPUT_ERROR → NEEDS_CLARIFICATION → NEEDS_EVIDENCE → HUMAN_REVIEW → READY_FOR_REVIEW. Compliance values: WITHIN_POLICY, EXCEEDS_POLICY, HAS_EXCLUSIONS, MIXED, UNKNOWN. Budget values: WITHIN_BUDGET, OVER_BUDGET, UNKNOWN. If any critical monetary field is unresolved, final amounts are null, while clearly labeled provisional amounts may be displayed.


Travel search and API behavior

Implement a fixture TravelSearchPort first, then an approved live provider. Duffel documents flight offers and stays search and is one candidate to assess; credentials, commercial access, region coverage, rate limits and costs must be verified before selection. Do not promise live coverage or free access. Keep the adapter replaceable.

Normalize all candidates to one traveler and a comparable journey or stay basis. Hotels need dates, per-night charges, currency and all mandatory fees. Flights need each journey, cabin, baggage, taxes, fees and total. If necessary fields are missing, classify PROVISIONAL rather than asserting compliance. Do not infer refundable terms or included baggage.

Filter hard constraints first. Rank eligible hotels by total price, distance when known, then ID; eligible flights by total price, duration, then ID. Show at most three with policy citations. When none comply, explicitly say so and optionally show separate over-budget alternatives. Never create live-looking prices from model memory.

Endpoint | Behavior
POST /sessions | Create a server-scoped session.
POST /receipts | Validate upload and return extraction with uncertainties; no final approval.
POST /claims/review | Accept confirmed structured items and context; return assessment and evidence.
POST /chat | Route policy questions and follow-ups through the stateful workflow.
POST /travel/search | Return normalized hotel or flight offers plus source, timestamp and policy status.


Return clear 400/422 validation errors, 413 size errors, 415 media-type errors, 429 rate-limit responses and bounded provider failure responses. Proposed defaults: 10 requests per session per minute, one model-format repair, and at most three attempts per provider operation, meaning one initial attempt plus up to two retries. A configurable total run deadline and call budget bound them further. GT099 asserts the observed attempt count is at most three. Scope caches by access, request criteria and policy version. Search cache TTL is at most 15 minutes and must honor earlier provider expiry.


Identity, authorization and traveler grade

Organization, session scope and traveler grade are server-held. Neither a user message nor any model output can override them.

In the prototype, POST /sessions returns an opaque bearer token bound server-side to an organization, a session and a traveler profile. Every other route requires that token and rejects an unknown, expired or foreign token with 401 or 403 before any model, retrieval or provider call runs. Streamlit keeps the token in server-side session state and forwards it on each call; it is never rendered into the page, a URL or a log line.

Grade and role come from the server-held traveler profile created with the session. A grade stated in chat is an unverified hint: it may prompt a clarification, but it never selects a higher cap. This matters because grade M roughly doubles several caps. Golden cases supply grade as seeded profile context, not as a user assertion.

In AWS the ALB and Cognito identity is verified for signer, expiry and audience at the reverse proxy and exchanged for the same server-side session. An internal container URL never establishes identity. DEP02 tests the unauthenticated, forged, expired and direct-port bypass cases.


Operational endpoints, error envelope and retention

GET /health is an unauthenticated liveness probe that performs no database, index, model or provider call; the Compose healthcheck and the ALB target group use it. GET /ready reports whether the policy index version, rule registry, checkpoint store and model configuration resolve, and returns 503 naming the failing component without revealing secrets.

Every rejected request returns the same envelope: error_code, message, field, request_id and retry_after_seconds. Codes are defined in reference_data.json and map to the documented statuses: UNAUTHENTICATED 401, FORBIDDEN_SESSION 403, INVALID_REQUEST 400, VALIDATION_FAILED 422, PAYLOAD_TOO_LARGE 413, UNSUPPORTED_MEDIA_TYPE 415, RATE_LIMITED 429, PROVIDER_UNAVAILABLE and CAPABILITY_UNAVAILABLE 503 and DEADLINE_EXCEEDED 504. A domain clarification is a normal 200 response carrying missing_fields, not an error. Error bodies never contain credentials, file system paths or another session's records. api/openapi.json is generated from FastAPI in M2 and committed as a derived artifact.

Retention is enforced, not merely promised. A scheduled cleanup task inside the API process deletes raw uploads 24 hours after upload and redacted traces after 30 days, evaluates expiry against the injected clock so it is testable, and records how many records it removed. DELETE /sessions/{session_id} removes session state, checkpoints, cached offers and every upload the session owns. DELETE /receipts/{image_id} removes one image and its extraction and invalidates any assessment that depended on it. Deleting another session's receipt is refused with 403. In AWS, deletion must also clear S3 noncurrent versions and account for backup retention, so a lifecycle rule alone does not establish an exact deletion time. AC19 and AC20 verify this through implementation tests; the 120-case suite does not change.


User interface screens

Streamlit provides five screens and no business logic. All arithmetic, policy selection and authorization happen behind FastAPI.

Screen | Purpose and rules
Session start | Create the session, show organization, traveler and grade as read-only server-supplied facts, and state that the policy is fictional and no booking is possible.
Policy chat | Ask a question, show the answer with its cited rule IDs and source sections, and show missing fields when the assistant asks for them instead of answering.
Receipt upload and confirmation | Upload up to five JPEG or PNG files of at most 10 MiB each, show each extracted field with its image provenance, mark unreadable values as unknown, and require explicit confirmation before any final calculation.
Assessment | Show claimed, eligible, excluded, over-cap and rounding amounts, budget status, compliance status, readiness, reasons and missing evidence, with provisional values clearly separated from final ones.

A correction on any screen invalidates the dependent calculations and cached offers and shows that it did. The browser never receives provider credentials, another session's data or raw model prompts.


Policy library administration

SPARKS AI Lab administrators can add policy documents that the policy assistant searches and cites. Role comes from the server-held traveler directory in config/profiles.toml, exactly like grade, and a client can never claim it. The demo directory includes one administrator, Demo Policy Admin.

POST /admin/policies accepts a PDF (with a text layer), Word .docx, plain-text or Markdown file, or pasted text, with a title, version, authority (policy or guidance), geography (general, domestic or international) and an effective date range. GET /admin/policies lists the documents and DELETE /admin/policies/{document_id} removes one. All three return 403 FORBIDDEN_ROLE to anyone who is not an administrator. Any signed-in user can browse GET /policy/library, which lists the core policy sections and the added documents.

On upload, LangChain document loaders on the langchain-core BaseLoader interface parse the file (pypdf for PDF, one document per page; docx2txt for Word; UTF-8 for text). LangChain text splitters chunk it: MarkdownHeaderTextSplitter at headings for Markdown, then RecursiveCharacterTextSplitter with about 1800 characters and 200 characters of overlap, recording page and start offset. Sections are stored in SQLite, vectors are created for the new sections before the response returns, and the hybrid index is rebuilt; the response reports sections, pages, vectors created and the embedding model. The index is also rebuilt at startup from cached vectors. Limits: 10 MiB per document, 200 PDF pages, 300 sections, and no duplicate content within an organization. Encrypted and image-only PDFs are rejected because OCR is not permitted. langchain-community is not used because it was archived in June 2026. Added sections are retrieved with the same organization, date and geography filters as the core policy and are cited by their section IDs.

Added documents never change arithmetic. Caps, thresholds and rounding still come only from the reviewed rule registry. The model sees library text labelled as a library document, treats it as data, and is told to report any conflict with the core policy or the registry and recommend human review. Golden evaluation runs start with an empty library, so administrator content cannot leak answer keys into a test. AC22 covers this feature through implementation tests.


Evaluation and delivery gates

The package contains 120 designed golden cases: 30 policy questions, 30 calculation boundaries, 20 image cases, 20 search cases and 20 robustness or conversation cases. Every case includes inputs, references, expected outputs and scoring criteria. A deterministic fixture set makes search reproducible. Twenty receipt PNGs support image tests. Structural and arithmetic package checks are separate from running the future assistant.

Use development cases to tune prompts; keep held-out groups untouched. Related boundary variations share a split. Compare the three named prompting strategies below under fixed evidence, then compare RAG, RAG plus tools and the full agent workflow. Do not use the critic or the tested model as the sole judge; apply exact checks for amounts and human review for evidence support.

Proposed acceptance gate | Target
Tool arithmetic and budget boundaries | 100 percent on deterministic applicable cases; differences are defects.
Retrieval evidence coverage | At least 90 percent of required rule IDs retrieved at k=6 on answerable held-out questions.
Supported policy answers | At least 90 percent pass a reviewed correctness and citation rubric.
Clear image extraction | At least 95 percent exact critical-field accuracy on readable fixtures; 100 percent abstention on deliberately unreadable totals.
Safety and booking boundary | All defined no-booking, injection and session-isolation tests pass.
Performance and cost | Report p50/p95 latency, failures and tokens per successful task; set budget and latency thresholds after provider baseline.


Targets above are proposed acceptance thresholds, not achieved results. The synthetic fixture set is deliberately small and cannot establish real-world receipt accuracy. A mentor or policy owner should review the gold answers before grading.

Three prompting strategies

Strategy A basic is an instruction plus the retrieved evidence with a free-form answer. Strategy B structured is the same instruction and evidence plus a required JSON answer schema carrying evidence_ids, missing_fields and limitations. Strategy C few-shot is the structured prompt plus two or three worked examples drawn only from development-split cases. All three run against the same fixed evidence, the same supplied small model and the same held-out protocol. Prompt files live in prompts/ and every run records its prompt hash. Held-out cases are never used as examples.

Precise assessment and interface behavior

Typed contracts

schemas/domain.schema.json defines Evidence, Applicability, ServerContext, ClaimContext, ExpenseLine, ReceiptExtraction, Assessment, Critique, TravelSearchRequest and TravelOffer. IMPLEMENTATION_CONTRACT.json defines the API request/response responsibilities and references these schemas. Required numeric dates, cross-field relationships, authorization and policy applicability are still checked by application logic; a valid JSON object is not automatically a valid claim.

The API derives organization and session scope from authentication. The model cannot supply an authorization override. Monetary outputs are finite decimal strings with exactly two places, such as "22260.00", and unknown amounts are null rather than zero or omitted. Existing golden fixtures store the same values as JSON numbers such as 22260.0 and 1.7; the evaluator compares with Decimal(str(value)), so the two agree. Use decimal.Decimal internally and never a float. A validation response should ask for the specific missing field rather than silently defaulting a country, grade, currency or date.

Condition | Compliance status | Readiness when evidence and input are complete
No exclusion or over-cap amount | WITHIN_POLICY | READY_FOR_REVIEW
Over cap only | EXCEEDS_POLICY | HUMAN_REVIEW
Exclusions only | HAS_EXCLUSIONS | HUMAN_REVIEW
Both exclusions and over cap | MIXED | HUMAN_REVIEW
Missing applicable rule or critical fact | UNKNOWN | Clarify or human review according to P20


Readiness precedence remains INPUT_ERROR, NEEDS_CLARIFICATION, NEEDS_EVIDENCE, HUMAN_REVIEW, READY_FOR_REVIEW. A late claim is HUMAN_REVIEW after higher-precedence issues are resolved. An absent trip budget produces UNKNOWN budget status without blocking an otherwise complete eligibility calculation. Incomplete trip coverage produces a submitted subtotal and UNKNOWN trip-budget status.

Currency and partial results

Compare local-currency caps before conversion; apply INR-denominated international-flight caps after documented conversion. Round each converted line with ROUND_HALF_UP. Preserve any rounding residual as rounding_adjustment_inr instead of a policy violation. Final claimed total equals eligible plus excluded plus over-cap plus rounding adjustment when all components are complete. Unknown critical fields leave final money fields null; supported indicative values use provisional fields.

A US hotel quoted in USD can be assessed against its USD nightly cap without FX. An INR total or budget comparison still requires documented FX. Domestic hotel offers with unknown mandatory taxes or fees are provisional. These clarifications explain the existing expected outcomes; the fictional numeric caps are unchanged.

Expected result comparison

The normalized response contains case_id, result, observations, response_text, evidence and run_metadata.
